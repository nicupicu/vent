Vaccination of Preterm Infants

  • Preterm = born <37 weeks gestation; extremely preterm = <28 weeks gestation.
  • Vaccinate according to chronological age (time since birth), not corrected/gestational age — with the same doses and schedule as full-term infants.
  • Despite immunological immaturity, preterm infants generally respond adequately to vaccines.
  • Medically stable infants who are ready for hospital discharge (regardless of weight) can generally receive routine vaccines at the appropriate chronological age.
  • Hepatitis B birth dose: if birth weight <2000 g and the mother is HBsAg-negative, the birth dose may be delayed until 1 month of chronological age or hospital discharge, whichever comes first; if HBsAg status is unknown or positive, HBIG plus HepB vaccine should not be delayed.
Source: Vaccination of Preterm Infants (Adel Reyad) & Catch-Up Immunization (Dr. Osama Hussein).

Post-Splenectomy / Asplenia (Including Sickle Cell Disease)

  • Anatomic or functional asplenia carries a lifelong high risk of overwhelming infection from encapsulated organisms — S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis, and H. influenzae type b.
  • Elective/planned splenectomy: give pneumococcal, meningococcal, and Hib vaccines ≥14 days before surgery whenever possible.
  • Emergency or already-performed splenectomy: give the same vaccines as soon as the patient is medically stable after the procedure — do not wait for an elective window.
  • Pneumococcal: one dose of PCV (13- or higher-valent) if not previously received as an adult, followed by PPSV23 ≥8 weeks later, then a PPSV23 booster 5 years after that first PPSV23 dose.
  • Meningococcal: complete the MenACWY and MenB series; because the risk is lifelong, booster doses are continued indefinitely (not a one-time series).
  • Hib: one dose for any unimmunized asplenic patient >59 months of age, or for anyone ≥15 months old who is unimmunized at the time of splenectomy.
  • Influenza: annual vaccination.
Source: CDC “Altered Immunocompetence” and Adult Immunization Schedule notes (2026), ACIP General Best Practice Guidelines.

Immunomodulator / Biologic Therapy (e.g. Tocilizumab/Actemra, Anakinra)

  • Tocilizumab (anti-IL-6) and anakinra (anti-IL-1) are both classed as immunosuppressive biologic therapies for vaccination purposes, in the same category as other TNF-alpha and interleukin inhibitors.
  • Live vaccines (MMR, MMRV, Varicella, Zoster [live formulation], Rotavirus, Yellow Fever, live intranasal influenza, BCG, OPV): avoid while on active therapy. Give ≥4 weeks before starting the biologic, or wait until ≥4 weeks after stopping it. Some manufacturer/travel-medicine sources are more conservative and use a 3-month post-cessation buffer — use the longer interval if there is any uncertainty about residual immune effect.
  • Inactivated vaccines (PCV/PPSV23, HepA/B, Tdap, IPV, HPV, injectable influenza, RZV/Shingrix, injectable COVID-19): can be given during therapy, but the immune response may be reduced. When feasible, give ≥2 weeks before starting the biologic for the best antibody response.
  • Update the patient's full routine and risk-based schedule (including annual influenza and, if age-eligible, Shingrix) before starting biologic therapy whenever the clinical situation allows.
Source: Australian Immunisation Handbook (2026) – biologic therapy vaccination timing table; tocilizumab prescribing information (AHFS DI, 2026).

Immunosuppression — High-Dose Systemic Steroids

  • Threshold considered immunosuppressive: ≥20 mg/day of prednisone (or equivalent) for adults/children >10 kg, or ≥2 mg/kg/day for children, given for ≥14 days.
  • Below this threshold — short courses (<14 days), low-to-moderate doses, alternate-day short-acting regimens, physiologic replacement dosing, or topical/inhaled/intra-articular routes — there is no vaccine restriction; live and inactivated vaccines can be given on the routine schedule.
  • At or above the threshold: avoid live vaccines during therapy. Wait at least 1 month after stopping high-dose steroid therapy before giving any live vaccine.
  • No interval is required before starting steroids after a vaccine, and inactivated vaccines never need to be delayed around steroid use — only live-vaccine timing is affected.
Source: CDC ACIP General Best Practice Guidelines for Immunization — Altered Immunocompetence chapter; CDC Yellow Book 2026, Box 2.2.2.

Post-Plasmapheresis / Plasma Exchange

  • There is no single numbered ACIP interval table specifically for plasmapheresis (unlike the blood-product tables below) — the guidance here reflects the general immunologic principle rather than one specific citation, so treat it as a practical framework rather than a fixed rule.
  • Plasma exchange removes circulating antibody, including recently vaccine-induced antibody, so a live vaccine given shortly before a plasmapheresis course may have its protective response blunted or removed.
  • Practical approach: avoid vaccinating in the 24–48 hours immediately before a scheduled plasmapheresis session.
  • If a live vaccine was recently given, ideally delay the start of a plasma exchange course until ≥2 weeks after vaccination when the treatment plan allows.
  • If plasmapheresis has already been completed, wait until the exchange course is finished, then reassess serologic status (where feasible) or apply standard antibody-blood-product intervals before giving live vaccines.
Source: general immunologic principle from CDC/ACIP antibody-blood-product interaction guidance; no dedicated numbered plasmapheresis table was located — verify locally with hematology/nephrology before acting.

Pre- and Post-Organ Transplantation

  • Pre-transplant (candidate evaluation): complete routine and catch-up vaccination as early as possible during work-up, before immunosuppression starts.
  • Live vaccines (MMR, Varicella, Rotavirus, Yellow Fever, BCG, OPV, live Zoster): give ≥4 weeks before transplantation; avoid live vaccines if transplant is expected within 4 weeks.
  • Inactivated/subunit/toxoid vaccines: give ≥2 weeks before transplant for the strongest antibody response.
  • Priority pre-transplant vaccines: complete or verify HepB series with post-vaccination anti-HBs titer, HepA series, pneumococcal (PCV plus PPSV23), meningococcal per risk factors, and Tdap.
  • Post-transplant: avoid vaccination in the immediate post-transplant period, when immunosuppression is most intense and the risk of a blunted response (or of triggering rejection-related concerns) is highest.
  • Live vaccines are generally contraindicated after transplantation except in specific, individualized circumstances.
  • Resume inactivated vaccines once maintenance (lower-intensity) immunosuppression is reached — typically 3–6 months post-transplant; influenza and COVID-19 vaccines can often be given earlier, as soon as 1 month post-transplant, if there is significant local disease activity.
  • Overall target for optimal timing across current guidelines: vaccinate ≥2 weeks pre-transplant OR ≥3 months post-transplant, avoiding periods of pulse immunosuppression or active rejection.
Source: IDSA 2025 Guidelines on Vaccines for Immunocompromised Persons; Stanford Guidelines for Vaccination of Adult Solid Organ Transplant Candidates/Recipients (2022); CDC Yellow Book 2026.

Post Blood Product Transfusion (Including Exchange Transfusion)

  • These intervals apply specifically to live measles- or varicella-containing vaccines (MMR, MMRV, Varicella) — inactivated vaccines never need to be delayed for this reason.
  • Washed red blood cells: no interval needed (negligible IgG content).
  • RBCs with adenine-saline added: 3 months.
  • Packed RBCs (hematocrit ≈65%) or whole blood: 6 months.
  • Plasma or platelet products: 7 months.
  • IVIG: interval depends on the dose/indication, roughly 8–11 months for higher-dose indications — see the IVIG interval calculator card above for the exact per-indication figure.
  • Exchange transfusion (e.g. neonatal double-volume exchange for hyperbilirubinemia or hemolytic disease) is not given its own separate numbered interval in general ACIP guidance; because it involves large-volume whole-blood/plasma exposure, the same whole-blood/plasma principle is applied in practice — defer live vaccines for at least 6 months (up to 7 if the exchange was primarily plasma) after the procedure.
  • If a live vaccine was given first, wait ≥2 weeks before giving any antibody-containing blood product when possible; if the product is given sooner, either repeat the vaccine after the interval above or check serology and revaccinate if seronegative.
Source: CDC ACIP General Best Practice Guidelines for Immunization, Table 2-4/3 (Blood products and antibody-containing products); CDC Pinkbook, Measles chapter (2026).
